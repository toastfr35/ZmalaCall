Recommendation: Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5 Development Kit (UK), £177.28 including VAT. It is the lowest-risk way to start the ARM/Linux port: 4 GB RAM, 32 GB soldered eMMC, dual-band Wi-Fi, strong CPU/GPU support, GPIO, two USB 3 ports, full-size HDMI, case, cooling, antenna, UK power supply, and cables are included. The seller showed it in stock. Its 32 GB eMMC meets the minimum in hw.md, although a later production carrier should use the 64 GB CM5 variant if update rollback space or logs make 32 GB tight.

Scoring method

The score is a project-specific assessment, not a general SBC benchmark. It weights Linux and driver maturity (30%), sustained CPU/GPU/media capability (20%), eMMC capacity and reliability (15%), Wi-Fi/USB/GPIO/display integration (15%), UK availability and product support (10%), and total value (10%). Every option still requires the acceptance tests in hw.md; a specification sheet cannot prove Liblinphone camera, codec, echo-control, and native-video behaviour.

Why the CM5 kit wins

  • It is a complete, currently stocked UK kit. CPC showed nine units in stock. The apparently cheaper CM5 module prices do not include a carrier, cooling, case, antenna, PSU, or cables.
  • Raspberry Pi publishes the carrier design, Linux documentation, GPIO interfaces, and long production commitment. CM5 and its IO board are stated to remain in production until at least January 2036.
  • The two USB 3 host ports cover a USB camera and USB conference speakerphone without consuming GPIO or adding a hub.
  • The 40-pin header directly supports the four active-low button inputs described in hw.md.
  • The CPU and graphics stack provide more margin than the ODROID-M1S while avoiding an external Wi-Fi adapter.

Purchasing choice: use the £177.28 including-VAT CM5 development kit for porting and hardware qualification. Businesses may save £1.78 including VAT through BCD Atlantik, whose web shop is explicitly business-to-business. If the application image cannot retain at least 8 GB free after reserving update/rollback space, move the production design to CM5104064 (4 GB RAM, 64 GB eMMC, wireless). Do not buy the 16 GB eMMC variant.

Other board checked

  • Raspberry Pi CM4: a 4 GB / 32 GB eMMC / wireless module plus IO board is inexpensive, but its 1.5 GHz BCM2711 is below the selection floor in hw.md and has less performance margin for software video coding. It is a fallback only after measurement.
